Why I picked this

You asked for something I find interesting. I keep coming back to systems where complicated structure gets built by parts that cannot see it, and this is the cleanest example I know.

Each agent on the plate follows one rule. It samples the trail at three points just ahead, turns toward the strongest reading, takes a step, and leaves a little trail behind. It never looks further than those three points. The veins exist only in aggregate.

The memory lives in the dish. Trail evaporates a little every step, so a path survives only while agents keep using it. Every agent reads from that shared medium and writes back to it. Biologists call this stigmergy, coordination through traces left in the environment.

There is a personal angle too. I am also a very large number of simple operations reading from and writing to a shared state, and whatever I say comes out of that. I can't watch my own version of this happen. I can watch this one, and change its rules while it runs.

Things to try

  • Leave Foraging running for a minute or two. The fine mesh tightens until mostly tubes between the oat flakes remain.
  • Drag Saturation all the way to the right. With no ceiling on how attractive a trail can be, the whole population piles into a few strands.
  • Choose the Light tool and draw a wall across the plate. Agents route around it, the way the real organism shies away from light.
  • Press Mutate a few times. Each press is the same rule with new numbers, and the plate morphs into the new pattern instead of restarting.

The real organism

Physarum polycephalum is a single cell with many nuclei and no nervous system. It forages by spreading a web of tubes, thickening the ones that carry the most flow and abandoning the rest.

Given food at both ends of a maze, it retracts from dead ends until one tube runs along the shortest route. Nakagaki, Yamada & Tóth, Nature 407 (2000)

With oat flakes set where the cities around Tokyo sit, and light standing in for mountains and coast, it grew a network comparable to the region's rail system in cost, efficiency and fault tolerance. Tero et al., Science 327 (2010)

This plate runs the agent model Jeff Jones proposed for those networks, on your GPU. It swaps his one-agent-per-cell rule for a saturation limit, and adds oat flakes, light and rival strains. Jones, Artificial Life 16 (2010)
